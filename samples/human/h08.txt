How do you explain technical problems to nontechnical people?
S: While working at ZBS POS, I often needed to explain the functions of an AI phone-ordering system and the limitations of large language models (LLMs), such as hallucinations and speech-recognition/STT noise, to Chinese restaurant owners without technical backgrounds. It was especially challenging with older couples running takeout restaurants.
T: I therefore needed to use the simplest analogies, as close as possible to their everyday lives or work. I generally used the steps of cooking in a kitchen as an analogy.
A: For example, sometimes customers requested custom features, and we needed to collect useful information from them to meet their needs. I would compare the process to cooking: what ingredients do we need before starting, what should we do at each step, what are the acceptance criteria at each stage, which easily overlooked details matter during the process, and what does success look like at the end? I explained these points to customers in concrete, easy-to-picture terms.
R: Restaurant owners understood the concept on the spot, gained a clearer understanding of how the product would be implemented, and felt more confident in it. Subsequent communication became much smoother.
