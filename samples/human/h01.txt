How do you deliver a product on time under a tight deadline? (Prioritization: MVP first, then supporting features, then cut nice-to-haves)
S: I remember my first hackathon. The challenge was to build a bilingual hospital-discharge follow-up product for the Latino community. We had six hours total, and my teammate and I planned to finish our respective parts and integrate them within five hours. At first, we were very relaxed, thinking we had plenty of time; we even ate snacks and chatted. We did integrate everything in the fifth hour, but when we started recording the demo, we found many problems. We panicked because we did not know how many issues there were or whether we could fix them within the remaining hour and submit on time according to the requirements. The presentation deadline was approaching.
T: We quickly discussed the situation and narrowed the demo scope to a version that we could demonstrate and whose limitations we could clearly explain.
A: I divided the features into three tiers: the essential demo path had to work; supporting features could be downgraded; and nice-to-haves would be cut outright. For example, we prioritized the path of "answering the call → bilingual functionality working correctly → answering basic customer questions accurately from the knowledge base." We postponed SMS confirmation and follow-up functionality and put them on a later to-do list.
R: We completed the demo on time and the core workflow worked. The cut features were recorded on the follow-up list. Since then, in subsequent projects, we have strictly managed time allocation. For time-limited projects, we allocate the first 30% of the time to completing the project, 50% to testing, and 20% to deployment and launch. If time is still tight, the most important thing is to cut scope first, protect the core workflow, list other supporting features as future work, and clearly disclose the limitations.
