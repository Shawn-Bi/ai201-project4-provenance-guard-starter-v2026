Tell me about a time you proactively discovered a problem without being asked to investigate it. What action did you take?
S: I remember when I was a channel sales manager at Vanke Real Estate. We had just finished an event in Beijing, and everyone was celebrating over dinner after a tiring day. The next day, an event would begin in Hangzhou. Major local partners would send sales representatives to the venue, invite their own customers, and register them in the system. My colleagues and I were responsible for several cities in East China, including Hangzhou. Because I was at the Beijing event and not in Hangzhou, other colleagues were going to manage the next day's Hangzhou event.
T: However, the company had just decided to adopt a new cooperation model with partners, so all the processes were new. During a break at dinner, I checked whether the cooperation workflow could run smoothly, to ensure the next day's Hangzhou event and events in other cities could proceed without problems. I discovered an issue in the customer-registration system provided to partners: partner organization names could not be found. As a result, registered customers could not be attributed to the relevant partner, potentially causing disputes when partners could not receive their commissions.
A: Concerned about the company's reputation and partners' trust, I repeatedly verified that the system problem was real and urgently contacted the relevant leaders. But it was already around midnight; the technical staff had finished work, and some had switched off their phones. I kept urging the responsible leaders to escalate and resolve the issue, explaining its seriousness. Eventually, I was told that the relevant technical personnel had been reached and were investigating and trying to fix the problem.
R: Fortunately, the back-office technical team got the workflow running before the event began the next day, so partners' interests and the company's reputation were not affected.
