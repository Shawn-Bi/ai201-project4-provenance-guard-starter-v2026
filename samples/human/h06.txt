How do you respond to harsh feedback?
S: While developing an AI solution for restaurants, a senior business leader pointed out that the AI prompts I had designed were "too conversational and too polite," making calls take too long and frustrating busy restaurant cashiers during peak hours.
T: In private or small-group discussions, I am extremely willing to hear anyone point out problems because I want products to improve by identifying and solving issues. But this feedback came during an all-company meeting, and I noticed an initial defensive reaction in myself. I needed to adjust the agent's behavior to fit demanding operational realities.
A: I listened carefully to the feedback, shadowed restaurant cashiers during peak hours, and analyzed call-duration data. I realized that in certain industries, brevity and efficiency are much more important than excessive empathy. So I rewrote the system prompts to be direct, efficient, and responsive.
R: As a result, average handling time per order fell by 28 seconds, order accuracy remained high, and feedback from restaurant operators improved dramatically.
Follow-up questions:
What was your initial reaction? Answer: Initially, I was defensive because I had believed that empathetic language and a conversation style as close to natural speech as possible were desirable. But rationally I knew he might be right, because different industries may prioritize different things and therefore need different styles. That style might be better suited to my previous AI mental health crisis product.
How did you handle the feedback? Answer: I translated operational observations directly into specific prompt-engineering constraints.
Did your relationship with the person change? Answer: Our relationship did not change. I realized in time that my reaction was just my feeling and did not mean the other person was wrong, so I focused as much as possible on solving the problem. The leader appreciated how quickly I responded and gave me greater autonomy in future prompt design.
