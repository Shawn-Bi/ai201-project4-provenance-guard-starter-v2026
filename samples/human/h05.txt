How do you make close collaboration work with someone whose working style is very different from yours?
S: During a training program, I was randomly assigned a teammate for a two-person AI project. I was responsible for the architecture and analytics module, while my teammate handled the content side. My style is to move quickly after designing the architecture and documenting responsibilities, resolving issues while delivering. She was very cautious and wanted to personally review every pull request (PR).
T: In just two days, I submitted ten stacked PRs, and she told me she felt overwhelmed. At first I was confused because, with advances in AI, I felt almost nobody reviewed code entirely on their own anymore; instead, they directed AI to assist with reviews. I suggested she try that method, but she did not adopt it. She also said my progress updates were confusing: she could not tell which items were her responsibility and which I had completed.
A: So I proactively adjusted my working style. I gave her time to review in her own way and labeled every to-do item with an owner, impact, proposed solution, and target completion date. Of course, I also set a deadline-based plan to ensure we could submit a demo: if her work showed no real progress by the cutoff, I would take full control of moving the project forward to ensure testing and the final demonstration were completed on schedule.
R: This adjustment worked. She began proactively communicating progress on her part and discussing the final demo flow with me. I also fully accepted her suggestions as long as they did not compromise product quality, turning them into fixes and regression tests. Ultimately, the entire two-agent workflow successfully ran end to end with real API calls. The process was not entirely perfect: as the deadline approached, I still had to complete the remaining content work on my own.
L: Now, I establish working agreements on the first day of collaboration, confirm early who is following up on which dependencies, and communicate in advance to understand how teammates prefer to work at critical milestones and in key processes.
